<h3>About me ✮⋆˙</h3>
<hr>
</div>
Hi
<img src="./Star_Rail_WORLD_Rappa.webp" width="250" align="left">
Hi!!
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;I'm Yao, a CSMayor, and I'm currently in my second year! I draw and program in my free time (which sounds funny when you consider that most of my homework and studying at home is programming anyway...)
<br>
I'm a huge fan of games! That's actually what got me into learning game development on my own. I started with Godot Engine, which I found absolutely mesmerising and incredibly engaging~ Since then, I've been working on my own games, as well as a Title I'm making together with a dear friend I met at university.
<br>
One of the first games I ever played was <i>Honkai Impact 3rd</i> by miHoYo. That's where my love for the franchise took root - and where I took my first steps towards the GameDev industry. Besides programming, I absolutely love drawing and bringing new characters to life!
<br>
But if, by this point, you're thinking:
<i>"Wow, what an enthusiastic person with such fond memories of discovering GameDev!"</i>
I need to stop you right there.
<br>
<b>I was brought to Scala hell.</b>
<br>
As much as I genuinely enjoy learning, and as much as Scala itself isn't actually that bad, I'm starting to develop a personal grudge against the: <i>Scala + collections + tail recursion + Apache Pekko combo.</i> You could say I have PTSD at this point.
<br>
(Just kidding. Mostly.)
<br>
I don't think anyone at my university was ready for it. But hey, at least now I can confidently say that I know a thing or two about Scala!
<br>
<i>Fun fact: Scala's creator, Martin Odersky, turned 67 this year. When we found that out, we cried (You know the drill...)</i>
<br>
I'm also part of the board of NaN++, a scientific club at my university. Being involved in it has given me a lot of opportunities to work with other people, and I've learned quite a bit about teamwork, collaboration, and keeping a project moving without everything spontaneously catching fire.
<br>
I'm a bit of a yapper and I absolutely love working on projects. However, I'm still a little shy when it comes to sharing my work, so I don't usually publish the full source code publicly. I'm planning to change that, though, and eventually create repositories where I can share my projects and their updates!
<br>
If you'd like to know anything else, don't hesitate to contact me - and thank you for taking the time to read this~
